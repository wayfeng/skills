# Question Design

## Questions

Use at least three of these types: action (how it works), contrast (why this over an alternative), causality (why it works), and boundary (where it fails).

Ask direct questions of 20 words or fewer. Each must be answerable from the source and create the need for the next question. Prefer “Why does X work?” or “Where does X break?” to definitions, tables of contents, generic pros-and-cons, or future speculation.

## Answers

Use exactly four parts:

```text
*Conclusion*: one standalone sentence
*Formalization*: one legible relation line
*Reasoning Steps*: 2-4 single-inference bullets
*Boundary*: conditions where the conclusion fails
```

Use plain words and simple symbols in formalizations, such as `old: X -> new: Y` or `A = B + C`. Ground jargon in concrete actions or objects. A boundary names failure conditions, not future work.

## Self-Check

Before final output, verify:

- [ ] Every Q resists one-line definition answers
- [ ] Every A has all four parts
- [ ] Conclusion line works out of context
- [ ] Formalization line is legible by itself
- [ ] Reasoning steps are one-inference-per-line
- [ ] Boundary states failure conditions
- [ ] Chain has direction: remove one Q and later Qs weaken
- [ ] At least three question types are used
- [ ] Each Q has 20 words or fewer
- [ ] Total count is 5-10
- [ ] Plain, direct, concrete English
