# Critical Reading

Analyze the source's reasoning and support, not agreement with its conclusion.

## Lenses

Trace: purpose, question, information, inference, assumptions, concepts, implications, and point of view.

Test relevant elements for clarity, accuracy, precision, relevance, depth, breadth, logic, significance, and fairness.

## Workflow

1. Identify the source, version, date, author, type, and material available. State the coverage limit.

**Complete when:** the analysis scope cannot be mistaken for a broader reading.

2. State the thesis and argument map in neutral language. Identify each reasoning element; label unavailable elements `Not established`.

**Complete when:** the argument can be reconstructed without adopting its conclusion.

3. Evaluate each applicable element against relevant lenses. Support every finding with a quotation, page, section, or passage.

**Complete when:** every strength and weakness has source evidence.

4. Steelman the argument, then give its strongest objections and plausible alternatives. Conclude what is supported, uncertain, unsupported, and what evidence would change the assessment.

**Complete when:** the verdict separates the source's claims from the analysis.

## Evidence Boundary

- `Author states`: explicit in the source.
- `Analysis infers`: supported interpretation.
- `Not established`: unsupported or unavailable from the material.

Verify externally checkable claims with primary sources when available; otherwise label them unverified. Cite only material actually available.

## Source-Type Checks

### Article

Check headline-body fit, context, source quality, framing, timeliness, and separation of fact, reporting, and opinion.

### Research Paper

Check research question, design, sample, measurement, controls, statistical and practical significance, uncertainty, reproducibility, limitations, conflicts, and conclusion-to-data fit.

### Book

Check scope, chapter-to-thesis coherence, representative examples, sources, counterarguments, conceptual consistency, and justification for prescriptive claims.

## Output

Use this structure; omit empty sections:

```markdown
# Critical Analysis: [Title]

## Scope
[Material analyzed and limitations]

## Thesis And Argument
[Neutral thesis, main premises, and conclusion]

## Reasoning Elements
| Element | What the source uses | Assessment |
|---|---|---|

## Quality Evaluation
### Strengths
- [Standard]: [evidence-based finding]

### Weaknesses
- [Standard]: [evidence-based finding and why it matters]

## Steelman And Challenges
[Strongest version, objections, and alternatives]

## Verdict
[Supported, uncertain, unsupported, and evidence needed]
```

Use calibrated terms: strong, adequate, weak, unsupported, and indeterminate. Assign a numeric score only on request.
